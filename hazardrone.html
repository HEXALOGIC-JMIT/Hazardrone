Rules that prevent the usual failures
  • Feeding the Pi 5 through its GPIO pins means it cannot detect a 5 A supply, so it limits USB ports to 600 mA unless you set usb_max_current_enable=1 (or PSU_MAX_CURRENT=5000). The UBEC must truly deliver 5 V at 5 A, and the C1 LiDAR and Pico hub draw from those USB ports.
  • CRSF/ELRS needs a true UART on the flight controller, not the RC-in pin, so the receiver goes on TELEM1. The 6C Mini has TELEM1, TELEM2, GPS1 and GPS2 as its serial ports.
  • Each 6C Mini port group is current-limited (1.5 A), so power the servo and Pi from the UBEC, not from the flight controller.
  • Use 3.3 V logic everywhere on the Pi and Pico. Feed the MQ-4 and MICS-6814 heaters from 5 V but bring their analog outputs down through a divider before the ADS1115.
  • Star-ground at the power module. Twist power and motor pairs, use shielded coax for the E-field probe, and keep the magnetometer and probe on the boom, away from battery and ESC current loops.
  • The Pi 5 camera port is a 22-pin connector, so the 15-pin Camera Module 3 needs a 22-to-15-pin ribbon.
  • Camera and I²C cables pass through the slot in the front bracket. The LiDAR USB lead is left slack so the tilting head cannot pull it.
  • The GPS time pulse (PPS) is only available if your GPS module breaks it out; the model leaves it out. Without it, stamp samples with Pi time disciplined by MAVLink GPS time.
  • The tilt servo on GPIO18: pigpio does not run on a Pi 5. Enable hardware PWM with dtoverlay=pwm-2chan (GPIO18 is PWM0 channel 2 on the Pi 5) and use a library that supports the RP1 chip. A hobby servo has no position feedback, so the commanded angle is not a measured angle; use a servo with a feedback pot or add an encoder if point-cloud accuracy matters.
  • The E-field amplifier and the mic preamp are powered from the Pico: 3V3 OUT and AGND run to each board (wires 30 and 31). Decouple each board with 10 µF + 100 nF, bring the shield to ground at the Pico end only, and use the Pico ADC_VREF and AGND pins as the analog reference. This is standard practice, not tested on this build.
  • One 5 V UBEC feeds the Pi, servo, LiDAR and both heaters. Servo stall current plus Pi load can approach the UBEC limit, so add a low-ESR capacitor at the servo supply, or give the servo its own BEC, and log Pi under-voltage flags. Estimate, not measured.
  • The Pixhawk 6C guide shows GPS1 as a 10-pin port and GPS2 as 6-pin. Confirm the 6C Mini pinout and buy the matching GPS cable.
  • The RPLIDAR C1 is rated ±30 mm and 6 m on dark targets, so it maps towers and pipes but cannot resolve millimetre shifts or thin conductors at range.

HAZARDRONE is a quadcopter that maps in 3D, finds broken power lines, thermal hotspots and gas or pressure leaks, and reports each fault at its 3D location. This tab says what each fault looks like to the drone and how the data becomes a finding.

Mapping and AI pipeline

  1. Acquire and time-sync. Every sensor sample is stamped with GPS time; the Pi records ROS 2 bags.
  2. Pose. ArduPilot EKF3 fuses GPS, IMU, barometer and compass; the tilt-servo angle completes the LiDAR transform. Scan matching (ICP or NDT in PCL or Open3D) corrects drift.
  3. Build the map. Accumulate scans, voxel-downsample to 5 cm, remove outliers, keep an OctoMap for obstacle avoidance.
  4. Segment. Cloth Simulation Filter removes ground, RANSAC fits planes, cylinders (pipes, poles) and conductors, DBSCAN separates objects.
  5. Compare to baseline. Align the new scan to the last one and compute per-point deviation in mm.
  6. Detect. Thermal thresholds, YOLO on RGB, 50 Hz lock-in and CUSUM on field data, FFT on ultrasound, gradient search on gas.
  7. Fuse and localise. Project each detection onto the 3D map by pose and timestamp, merge repeats of the same fault.
  8. Score and report. Severity = measured size × asset criticality × trend; the engineer gets a ranked list and verifies in person.

The Pi handles acquisition and light detection in flight; heavy mapping and model runs happen on a laptop or cloud after landing, which keeps the on-board hardware cheap.

Honest limits. Keep the sensor boom the legal and safe clearance from live conductors (set by the utility, often several metres or more; start with 11–33 kV lines). Motor and battery currents create magnetic noise, so the boom, a 50 Hz band-pass and calibration on a known-live line are needed. Propeller and ESC noise reach ultrasonic bands, so leak listening is done in a slow hover and treated as screening. Thermal at 32×24 pixels finds big hotspots, not small ones. Every finding is a flag for a qualified person to check.

Corrections to my earlier version

  • The Pi 5 powered from GPIO pins limits USB to 600 mA unless you set usb_max_current_enable=1; added to the wiring rules (Raspberry Pi USB-PD white paper).
  • ELRS/CRSF receivers need a true UART on the flight controller; the 6C Mini has TELEM1, TELEM2, GPS1 and GPS2 (ArduPilot docs). I moved the receiver to TELEM1 and the TF-Luna to GPS2.
  • A GPS time pulse is not on every module; the wiring tab now says so.
  • The Teensy price was my guess; the Pico 2 replaces it with a listed price. The total is now ₹1,08,825.
  • Some cables were missing before (servo, LiDAR, camera ribbon, thermal, gas pod, rangefinder). All 31 connections are now drawn (wires 30 and 31, the analog supply for the E-field amp and mic preamp, were missing), and the page checks that every wire end lands on a real port.
  • The Pico ADC pins now sit where the real board has them: GP26 and GP27 are adjacent pins on one header row, with 3V3 OUT further along it. The Pico standoffs also sat 20 mm past the board and are fixed.
  • Dashboard span labels now match the map: the break is in span T7–T8, not "6–7".
Pitch line this supports: big utilities already inspect with LiDAR, thermal and zoom drones, so do not claim to replace them. HAZARDRONE is a low-cost, open, India-buildable platform that adds line-fault and leak sensing to that workflow. The funding proves two things: the field-sensing method on a real line, and the sensor fusion and dashboard.

9. Assembly order
  1. Bolt arms and standoffs between the two plates; fit motors and check the arrow on each prop hub (red ring CCW, blue ring CW).
  2. Fix ESC and power module on the bottom plate; solder the motor wires; check motor spin direction before props go on.
  3. Mount the flight controller on foam, GPS on its mast, receiver and TF-Luna under the plate.
  4. Mount the Pi on four standoffs and the Pico beside it; fit the camera bracket, cameras and mic.
  5. Build the LiDAR post, plate, servo and bracket; fit the gas pod and the E/B boom.
  6. Run each wire from the connection list; tie down with the slack shown.
  7. Strap the battery, slide it to balance, and measure the centre of gravity before the first flight.
